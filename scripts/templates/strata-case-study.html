Project 02  /  Portfolio construction & risk

Strata

A portfolio construction and risk system: build an allocation from any set of tickers, or let an optimizer solve for one, then see what the rule earned out of sample, what it cost to run, and the range of outcomes that could follow.

The verdict: of four allocation policies on eight TSX stocks, Balanced earned the best risk-adjusted return (Sharpe {bal_sharpe} against {xic_sharpe} for XIC) with a shallower worst loss than the index. Growth's extra return came with a {growth_dd} drawdown. A minimum-variance optimizer delivered the lower volatility it promised but lost to plain equal weighting on Sharpe ({mv_sharpe} against {ew_sharpe}) while trading {mv_turnover_multiple}× as much.

Optimizer lost out of sample

CAD 100,000 per portfolio from {first} to {last}, rebalanced monthly to target, 10 bps on traded notional. Optimizers re-solve at each rebalance from the previous 252 sessions and are judged only on the returns that followed. Benchmark: XIC.TO, held without rebalancing.

The thirty-second version

1

Balance beat concentration on risk-adjusted terms

Balanced returned {bal_ann} a year against XIC's {xic_ann} at almost the same volatility, and its worst loss was {bal_dd} against the index's {xic_dd}. One sample and one universe, so this is an observed result, not evidence of persistent skill.

2

Growth's return came with half its value at risk

A 65% technology target compounded at {growth_ann} a year but fell {growth_dd} from peak. In five-year scenarios it drops below 80% of the starting balance in {growth_below80} of paths, against {bal_below80} for Balanced, and finishes behind Balanced in {growth_under} of shared market paths.

3

Optimizing did not beat equal weight

Minimum variance achieved lower volatility ({mv_vol} against {ew_vol}) but a Sharpe of {mv_sharpe} against {ew_sharpe} for equal weighting, with {mv_turnover_multiple}× the turnover. An optimal answer to estimated inputs is not the best realized portfolio; the system reports that instead of assuming the sophisticated method wins.

02

The application

Build a portfolio, test it, see where it could land

Strata is the deployed version of the research: enter Yahoo Finance tickers and weights, or pick one of twelve registered construction methods, and a single run returns the realized history, the costs, a drawdown profile and 5,000 simulated five-year paths. Up to four portfolios run side by side in the same simulated markets, so differences come from construction rather than a luckier draw.

BUILD

Portfolios & strategy

Tickers and weights, or a registered method: minimum variance, risk parity, maximum diversification, maximum Sharpe and their convex variants.

MANDATE

Constraints

Position caps, sector and country limits, turnover budgets, tracking-error ceilings, and optional spread and impact costs.

RISK

Risk models

Sample covariance, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, or a statistical factor model whose factor count follows the Marchenko-Pastur noise edge.

REPLAY

History & drawdowns

Monthly rebalancing with daily drift, costs on traded notional, rolling one- and three-year return ranges, depth of every drawdown.

SIMULATE

Five-year scenarios

5,000 block-bootstrap paths shared across portfolios, head-to-head comparisons in the same markets, and sampling intervals on each percentile.

RECORD

Run records

Every result carries an identifier built from the request, the price data and the source code, with library versions, and exports in full.

One comparison, several objectives. Growth leads on return, Balanced on Sharpe, and Low volatility on realized volatility ({lv_vol} against XIC's {xic_vol}). Each answers a different investment question, so the useful output is the trade-off, not a winner. Income shows why a label needs testing against holdings: banks, utilities and energy gave it XIC's return and a smaller drawdown with no meaningful volatility advantage. Adjusted prices approximate reinvested dividends; judging it as an income strategy would need a cash-distribution ledger.
How much is the benchmark choice doing? XIC is the natural same-currency reference, but these portfolios hold eight names in five sectors against an index of more than 200, so every gap mixes sector exposure with security selection. They are observed differences for these chosen names, not isolated manager skill.
04

Did optimizing help?

Optimized weights, judged only on what came next

Beyond fixed targets, Strata re-solves the weights at every monthly rebalance from the previous 252 sessions, using Ledoit-Wolf covariance and a 35% cap per holding, and holds them over returns the optimizer never saw. Equal weighting on the same dates is the honest control: it needs no estimates at all.

{construction_rows}
Construction ruleAnnual returnVolatilitySharpeTurnover a yearCosts paid
Minimum variance did its job and still lost. It ran at {mv_vol} volatility against equal weighting's {ew_vol}, but its Sharpe of {mv_sharpe} trailed equal weighting's {ew_sharpe}, and re-solving every month cost {mv_cost} against {ew_cost}. The app shows why on a longer 2017–2026 run of the same rule: the risk model expected 8.7% volatility and the portfolio realized 14.5%. Estimated covariance understates risk exactly when correlations rise. This is consistent with the estimation-error problem studied by DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009), not a replication of it.
Risk parity was the middle road. It matched minimum variance's volatility ({rp_vol}) with a Sharpe of {rp_sharpe}, close to equal weighting, at the lowest turnover of the optimized rules. My ETF research pipeline runs the same class of optimizer on a less correlated six-ETF universe and reaches the opposite conclusion about whether optimizing helps, which is the point: the answer depends on the universe, not on the method's sophistication.

The sample contains an unusually strong decade for Canadian equities, so these are ranges under a historical-repeat assumption, not forecasts, and they have not been validated against unseen five-year outcomes.

Accounting conventions

Portfolio return

rp,t = ∑ wi,t−1 ri,t

Weights from the previous close. End-of-day weights would let today's move choose the allocation that earned it.

Trading cost

cost = V × ∑ |wtarget − wdrifted| × 0.001

10 bps on total traded notional, purchases and sales, charged before the next return.

Annualized return

CAGR = (Vend / Vstart)252/N − 1

On net portfolio value over N observed daily returns.

Sharpe ratio

mean(r − rf) / s(r) × √252

A fixed 3% annual risk-free rate, converted to a daily compound rate.

Drawdown

Dt = Vt / max(V0…Vt) − 1

Starting capital counts as the first peak, so an immediate loss registers.

Scenarios

5,000 paths × 1,260 sessions

Blocks of 20 completed net returns, the same block draws for every portfolio; costs are embedded in the history sampled.

The data mart holds securities, security_daily_analytics, portfolio_positions, portfolio_daily_summary and pipeline_runs. The analysis queries use CTEs, ROW_NUMBER for month-end NAV, LAG for compounded returns and DENSE_RANK for volatility ranking.

07

Research to production

A contract between a researcher's rule and running software

Each construction method is a function from a context to a weight vector, registered with metadata. The API advertises the registry, the interface builds its menu from it, and the walk-forward loop calls the same contract, so a new method inherits every mandate limit and check without knowing they exist.

Conformance gate

Fifteen checks on every method

Valid weights, respected constraints, repeatable results, asset-order invariance, degenerate inputs and a solve-time budget. It runs in CI and fails the build; tests feed it deliberately broken methods to prove it rejects them.

12 of 12 registered methods pass

What the gate caught

Defects with investment consequences

A maximum-Sharpe solver that blew its 750 ms budget with redundant restarts, mandate parameters leaking between objectives, and a ratio transform whose answer changed with the units of the returns.

Each now covered by a regression test

Provenance

Every result has a run record

An identifier built from the request, the price data and the source code, with library versions. Two results are the same only if their records match, not because their links do.

Checksummed inputs · run manifest

Operations

Honest failure modes

Runs report progress, reuse cached results that match exactly, and surface data-source failures rather than silently substituting frozen prices. Solver status and constraint duals are reported, so infeasible is never shown as optimal.

08

Validation

Tests aimed at the errors that would change the conclusion

  • Each day's return is earned by the weights held at the previous close
  • Low-volatility weights come from the first 252 sessions only; rewriting later prices leaves them unchanged
  • Rewriting the final month of prices leaves every earlier optimized weight unchanged
  • Scenarios start after the last completed backtest date and sample only completed returns
  • Positions reconcile to NAV before the data mart is published
  • Paired scenario differences are tested against subtracting separate percentiles
  • Three independent implementations of two optimizers agree, including a MATLAB cross-check under Octave
  • A 10% loss then a 10% gain compounds to a 1% loss; costs reduce final value
What the tests cannot remove. The eight stocks and the four policies were chosen with hindsight, so results carry selection and survivorship bias even with correctly timed weights. Adjusted prices are today's snapshot, not a point-in-time archive, and the constant risk-free rate and flat costs are simplifications. {tests} automated tests across the research pipeline and the application run in GitHub Actions with the conformance gate, the MATLAB cross-check, a synthetic pipeline run and the demo build.
09

Next research iteration

Test the downside harder before trusting the upside

Rerun the scenarios under explicit adverse regimes and other historical windows, add a trading calendar and coverage audit, scale the market-impact model by average daily volume, replace the cardinality heuristic with a mixed-integer program, and use point-in-time universes once membership and delisting data are available. If an allocation only looks attractive under one set of assumptions, that dependence belongs in the decision.

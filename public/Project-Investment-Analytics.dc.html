The research question

How do different equity allocations change returns, risk and sector exposure, and can a simple statistical model improve on a trailing-volatility forecast?

Growth emphasizes technology. Income holds banks, utilities and energy. Balanced diversifies across equity sectors. Low volatility uses inverse-volatility weights estimated before the evaluation period. All four are compared against XIC, a Canadian equity ETF, with consistent dates and financial assumptions.

From source data to research outputs

  • Extract: historical adjusted prices, portfolio weights and security-sector metadata.
  • Validate: detect missing prices, conflicting duplicates, unknown holdings and invalid allocations.
  • Analyze: calculate returns, volatility, Sharpe ratios, drawdown, sector exposure and benchmark differences using pandas and NumPy.
  • Simulate: let weights drift between monthly rebalances and deduct costs on traded notional.
  • Model: compare linear regression, Ridge, Lasso and ElasticNet for next-20-session volatility.
  • Publish: load SQLite tables and views, export CSVs, and generate the report and executed notebook.

Validation before prediction

The final 20% of observations is held out. Time-series cross-validation includes a 20-session gap so training labels cannot overlap validation periods. Scaling is fitted inside each training fold, and model selection uses development data only.

In this run, the selected Ridge models had lower test error than persistence for three portfolios, but not Income. All four had negative test R-squared. Those results are included alongside the charts: the experiment measures whether prediction helps, without assuming it will.

Engineering and financial assumptions

The SQL layer demonstrates joins, common table expressions, aggregations and window functions. Tests check compounding, initial losses, allocation reconciliation, transaction costs, future-data invariance and database publication failures. Frozen inputs, checksums, configuration and run history support reproduction.

Prices are dividend- and split-adjusted research data. The chosen universe introduces selection and survivorship bias. Income describes the sector allocation, not a modeled dividend stream. Forecasts are a separate experiment and do not change portfolio holdings.

Tools: Python, pandas, NumPy, SQLite, scikit-learn, Plotly, matplotlib, Jupyter and pytest.
